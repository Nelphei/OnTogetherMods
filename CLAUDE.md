# On-Together mods

BepInEx 5 plugins for On-Together (GigaPuff), a Unity 6 (6000.0.60) **Mono** game. Multiplayer runs on PurrNet over Steam lobbies, with Vivox voice.

## Layout
- `Directory.Build.props`: shared settings for every mod. Targets netstandard2.1, sets the game path (`GameDir`), and references BepInEx, HarmonyX and every game/Unity DLL with `Private=false`.
- `Directory.Build.targets`: when built with `-p:DeployToGame=true`, copies the DLL and PDB to `<GameDir>\BepInEx\plugins\<AssemblyName>\`. Normal builds don't touch the game.
- `HelloTogether/`: starter mod (a logging plugin plus one Harmony postfix). Copy it to start a new mod, change the GUID and names, delete its `<NoDeploy>true</NoDeploy>` line (it keeps the template out of the game when deploying), then `dotnet sln add`.
- `PhotoMode/`: free camera, UI hiding and PNG screenshots (F9 to toggle). Works only in the Normal window mode.
- `EmoteSync/`: players who turn it on (F7) start emotes together on the same network tick, and join each other's free-focus activities with loops kept in step (a busy player joins once free, for up to 30 s, or at once with a "Join now" button). Every player in the group needs the mod.
- `TeleportSpots/`: a window (F5) where you save the spot you're standing on and click a name to teleport back there. Spots are saved in `BepInEx\config\TeleportSpots.json`. Only you need the mod.
- `SongPlayer/`: plays .mid files on the instrument you're sitting at. Its window appears by itself at an instrument. Songs go in `Music\On-Together`. Other players hear the song without the mod.
- `TraceOverlay/`: while you draw on a chalkboard or whiteboard, lays a see-through picture over the board to trace. Its window appears by itself while drawing. Images go in `Pictures\On-Together\Trace`. Only you see the picture.
- `FocusAnywhere/`: the focus key opens the free focus menu on any ground or in the air (you stop there and focus floating), and that menu lists every activity in the game (land, water and seat-spot ones). Seat spots keep their own menu. Its icon switches the whole mod off and on; switched off, it writes the game's own activity lists back once you're not focusing (`ActivityList.Restore`). Only you need the mod.
- `DeskSnap/`: when you sit at your desk near someone else's, moves you beside it, facing the same way, at exactly their height (so rows stay level, even in the air), with a small gap (or to the end of the row). F3 turns it on/off, and while sitting also snaps you or moves you back. Also snaps after an EmoteSync focus join. Only you need the mod.
- `ChatRange/`: a small box just above the chat box (beside it if there's no room) listing who's in range of your local chat messages. Shows while the chat box is open on its Local tab. Not in the PanelStack stack, since it follows the chat box. Only you need the mod.
- `NameDesigner/`: a window (F4) to colour each letter of your player name or a session name, with a TextMeshPro preview and 5 preset slots per tab (saved in `BepInEx\config\NameDesigner.json`). On the main menu it opens by itself beside the Create Session box and writes the design straight into it. A player name can also colour the colon after it in chat: the name ends with `</color><#colon>`, which closes chat's player colour and leaves the colon colour open for the game's own `:</color>` (see `Design.ToRichText`; text the game puts after a name elsewhere takes that colour too). Only you need the mod; everyone sees the colours.
- `OutfitSlots/`: outfit slots 4-9 on top of the game's 3, as extra rows in the outfit screen's style list (copied from row 3), plus a quick-swap window (F2; right-clicking the icon wears the next slot). Slots 4-9 are saved in `BepInEx\config\OutfitSlots.json`, and the game's own save only ever sees slots 1-3 (see the outfits note). Stays off while BlueSage QoL's own style slots 4-9 are on (`EnableExtendedAvatarStyles`), and copies them on its first run. Only you need the mod; everyone sees the outfit.
- `QuickHide/`: in split screen and mini mode, a key (F10) or its icon makes the whole game window invisible and click-through (world, characters, all UI), and the key brings it back, even while another app is focused (read with `GetAsyncKeyState`, Windows only). Hiding from inside the game hands the keyboard to the next app window down (`Win32.FocusWindowBehind`), so typing doesn't reach the hidden game. Picking the game in the taskbar or Alt+Tab, switching window modes or leaving the world shows it again. Does nothing in Normal mode. Only you need the mod.
- `NetworkCrashFix/`: stops a crash in PurrNet's packet reader (see the crash files note). Prefixes on `BitPacker.ReadBitsWithoutChecks` (which `ReadBits` and `ReadBytes` call) and `ReadBits` read byte by byte when within 9 bytes of the array's end, with bytes past it counted as 0; everywhere else the game's code runs. Before patching it compares its reader with the game's for every start bit and bit count, and stays off on a mismatch; after patching it checks `ReadBits` and `ReadBytes` really go through it. Always on: no key, no in-game icon and no on/off setting (removed in 0.1.1; `Settings` deletes the old `Enabled` / `ShowIcon` lines from the .cfg). Its `Icon.cs` isn't compiled and is only there for the Thunderstore icon. Only you need the mod.
- `DeskSwarm/`: a window (F11) to fly your seated desk (`Flight.Pilot`: movement keys camera-relative, Space up, Shift down, tilt into the motion) and to lead or join swarms. Followers' desks fly to their spot in the leader's formation (Ring, V, Line, Tower, Trail; `Formations.Place`), worked out on each follower's own screen from the leader's pose, their place in the leader's list and the shared tick clock. Membership goes over Steam lobby chat like EmoteSync (tag `OTDS1`; only the leader changes a swarm and sends it whole as `SWARM`). Moves only free-focus sitters (not seat spots), always your own character, through `Game.MoveSeated`. While flying, the stand-up key is blocked (`InputPatch`) and the game's camera drift is off. Standing up away from the sit spot sends the missed trigger exits (`AreaExits`). Only you need it to fly; everyone in a swarm needs it.
- `Shared/`: source files compiled into more than one mod, each project adding them with `<Compile Include="..\Shared\X.cs" Link="Shared\X.cs" />`. `PanelStack.cs` stacks each mod's window along the bottom, right edges lined up 15% in from the right (clear of the chat box). Call `PanelStack.Place(id, order, size)` every OnGUI, skip drawing while `PanelStack.Hidden` is true (Photo Mode is taking a photo, or the game's eye button has hidden its UI; see the window modes note), and add an order constant for each new mod. `ClickBlocker.cs` is the invisible game-UI blocker that goes under a mod window (see the last game note). `IconBar.cs` puts each mod's 16 x 16 pixel-art icon (the mod's own `Icon.cs`, one palette letter per pixel, palette in `IconBar.cs`) in a row in the bottom-right corner: click does the mod's key, right-click a second action, hover shows name and key, a green dot means on and greyed out means off. Each mod (all but NetworkCrashFix) calls `IconBar.Draw(...)` first thing in OnGUI every frame (so its control id stays put), acts on the returned click in Update, calls `IconBar.Patch()` in `Plugin.Awake` (the first mod to load patches input for all of them), and has a `ShowIcon` setting; add an order constant for each new mod. The row shows only in the world while no game menu is open (see the menus note) and Photo Mode is off (it calls `IconBar.HideRow()` every frame), shares one click blocker, and PanelStack lifts the windows above it when it reaches under them. Mods without a key (SongPlayer, TraceOverlay, ChatRange, FocusAnywhere) use their icon as a remembered on/off switch (`Enabled`). `TextBox.cs` is a dark text box drawn with TextMeshPro on its own canvas, for any mod text with player names in it (see the player names note); `PlayerNames.cs` gets names ready for it.
- `Thunderstore/`: `pack.py` makes a Thunderstore zip per mod in `Thunderstore\bin\` (`python Thunderstore/pack.py [Mod ...]`, or the "package for Thunderstore" task). It does a clean Release build with no PDB path, renders `icon.png` from the mod's `Icon.cs`, builds `README.md` from the mod's row and section in the repo README (with icon and `ShowIcon` notes only if the .csproj compiles `IconBar.cs`), and stops if the version in the .csproj, Plugin.cs and the changelog don't match, or if your Windows user name is in any file. Package names, descriptions, team and BepInExPack dependency are in `mods.json`; each mod's changelog is `changelogs\<Mod>.md`. A new mod needs an entry in both.
- `reference/Assembly-CSharp/`: the game's code decompiled with ilspycmd, one file per class. It's gitignored and must never be published. Re-generate after a game update:
  `dotnet ilspycmd -p -o reference/Assembly-CSharp -r "<Managed dir>" "<Managed dir>/Assembly-CSharp.dll"`, then delete the generated `.csproj`.
  The ilspycmd version is pinned to 9.1.0.7988 because newer releases need a .NET SDK newer than 8.

## Workflow
- Write and build mods here in the repo, and commit them to git. Output goes to each project's `bin\`.
- Deploy to the game only when the user says it's ready for testing. Don't deploy as a side effect of building.

## Build / test
- Build: `dotnet build`, or Ctrl+Shift+B in VS Code. This stays in the repo.
- Deploy: `dotnet build -p:DeployToGame=true`, or the "deploy to game" task in VS Code (Terminal > Run Task). Add a project path to deploy one mod only, e.g. `dotnet build PhotoMode -p:DeployToGame=true`.
- Close the game before deploying a mod it has already loaded, because the DLL is locked.
- Logs: `<GameDir>\BepInEx\LogOutput.log` and `%USERPROFILE%\AppData\LocalLow\GigaPuff\On-Together\Player.log`. The BepInEx console window is enabled.

## Game notes (learned while building PhotoMode)
- Searching `reference/`: shell `grep pattern *.cs` breaks, because the file `-PrivateImplementationDetails-.cs` is read as an option. Use the Grep tool, or `grep -- pattern *.cs`.
- Singletons: `MonoSingleton<T>.I` / `NetworkSingleton<T>.I` call `FindAnyObjectByType` whenever the instance is missing (null on the main menu). Don't call them every frame outside a lobby.
- Input: `InputManager` reads the legacy `UnityEngine.Input` and exposes properties. Patching `TaskManager.IsCancelInputs` to return true zeroes all of them (chat uses this). Enter still gets through and opens chat.
- Camera: Cinemachine 2 (`using Cinemachine`). Disabling the `CinemachineBrain` on `Camera.main` gives you manual control (`SwanController` does this). `PlayerMovementController.SetScreenCameraRotation` handles right-click look, scroll zoom and cursor unlocking.
- Window modes: only `OverlayManager.ResolutionMode == Normal` renders the world directly to the screen. Landscape, Portrait and Desk render into a texture behind UI masks. Keys 1-4 pick Normal, Landscape, Portrait and Desk (`OverlayManager.Update`). All three make the game window a see-through, always-on-top layer over the desktop: Landscape and Portrait ("split screen") show the world in a strip (a third of the height at the top or bottom, or a quarter of the width at the left or right), and Desk ("mini mode") in a small picture. Mod UI is still placed against the whole screen, so parts of it can float over the desktop. Every mode, Normal included, has an eye button (top-right in Normal; `UIManager.ButtonHideUI`, private `_hideUIState`: Open → Hide → Open, with an extra HideCompletely step in Desk) that hides the game's UI, and every mod's UI hides with it (`PanelStack.Hidden`, `PanelStack.GameUIHidden`). Switching modes doesn't reset it. The see-through window is Kirurobo's UniWindowController (`using Kirurobo;`, `UniWindowController.current`): `alphaValue` fades the whole window, and every frame its hit test (`isHitTestEnabled`, the opacity of the pixel under the mouse) switches `isClickThrough`, so turn the hit test off to keep clicks going through. Leaving the world switches back to Normal first (`MainSceneManager`). The game is borderless fullscreen in these modes and keeps running while another app is focused.
- `SettingsController` uses its Canvas `enabled` flag to mean the menu is open, so don't toggle it.
- Name tags and chat bubbles: `TextBubbleController._textBubbleCanvas`.
- Player names: `PlayerController.PlayerNameText` is a TextMeshPro text, and players can put TextMeshPro rich text tags in their names (colours, sizes, gradients...). Unity's IMGUI shows those tags as code, so any mod text with a player name in it goes in a `Shared/TextBox.cs` box: TextMeshPro with the name tag's font, measured and drawn from OnGUI like IMGUI, hidden from LateUpdate. `PlayerNames.ForText` closes tags a name leaves open. The game keeps each player's Steam ID next to their name tag: `PlayerPanelController.PlayerSteamIDs[i]` goes with `PlayerControllers[i]` (EmoteSync's `Game.PlayerName`). Photo Mode hides the game's UI by switching off screen canvases, but skips mod canvases (named with `PanelStack.CanvasPrefix`), which hide themselves while a photo is taken.
- Chat box: not its own canvas. It's several `UIManager` objects (`_messagePanelParent` is the draggable container) on the main UI canvas. `IsMessagePanelHidden` is true when the player has minimised chat. PhotoMode shows it alone by turning the canvas back on and adding switched-off Canvas components to every non-chat sibling. It keeps that canvas's raycasters off, because UI hidden that way probably still catches clicks, so it checks clicks on the chat box (`UIManager.MessageInputField`) itself. Pressing Enter selects the chat box (`UIManager.Update`), and the box sends its message on submit (`TextChannelManager.OnEnterPressed`). Local chat messages and chat bubbles reach players within 5 m (3D): each receiving game compares the position the sender sent with its own (`OnChannelMessageReceived`, `TextBubbleController.ShowTextBubble`). `TextChannelManager.Islocal` is true while the Local tab is selected.
- Scenes and menus: build index 1 is the main menu and 2 is the world (`SceneManager.GetActiveScene().buildIndex`, cheap to check every frame). In the world, every game menu or panel sets `TaskManager.LockState`: `UILock` (pause menu, emote wheel), `UIWalkable` (shop, player status), `UIInventory` (inventory), `UIFishUI` (focus menu, player list, fishing), `Music` (at an instrument or drawing board). `Free` means none is open, including while seated focusing.
- Right-click: while `InputManager.IsMouseButton1` is held, `PlayerMovementController.SetScreenCameraRotation` turns the camera and locks the cursor, then warps it back on release, so an IMGUI right-click ends at the wrong spot. IconBar clears `IsMouseButton1` / `IsMouseButton1Up` for a press that starts on its row.
- Hotkeys: the game uses no F keys. BlueSage uses F8 and Steam screenshots use F12. PhotoMode uses F9 and F1. EmoteSync uses F7 and F6. TeleportSpots uses F5. DeskSnap uses F3. NameDesigner uses F4. OutfitSlots uses F2. QuickHide uses F10. DeskSwarm uses F11, which was the last free F key.
- Animations: every player animation is one networked animator int, `AnimState`, set through `PlayerAnimationController` (owner only, via PurrNet `NetworkAnimator`, sent once per tick). Emotes: `EmoteController.SetEmoteAnim(index)`, with the anim value in `GameSettings.EmoteAnimStates`. Focus: `PlayerFocusController.SetFocus` also handles props, desk, camera, timer and seats.
- Mod-to-mod messages: the game never uses Steam lobby chat (`SendLobbyChatMsg` / `LobbyChatMsg_t`), so EmoteSync uses it as a channel that unmodded players never see. Don't use lobby member data for this: changing it makes PurrLobby refresh the whole lobby. The lobby ID is `MultiplayerManager.LobbyCode`.
- Shared clock: `PurrNet.NetworkManager.main.tickModule.syncedPreciseTick`. The host counts ticks and clients re-sync to it using their ping.
- Moving the player: the owner sets `transform.position` (the game does this when you fall off the world). Keep the `CharacterController` on and call `Physics.SyncTransforms()`, because a disabled collider never fires `OnTriggerExit` for area triggers. The player's `NetworkTransform` is owner-controlled and sends the move to everyone on the next tick (as a change from the last value sent). Never call `ForceSync()` on a player: when a non-host calls it, the host relays the move again as a change on top, so other non-hosts see the player go twice as far and turn twice as much (PurrNet's `ForceSyncServer` doesn't reset the host's `_lastSentDelta`). The game never calls it for players. The camera follows `MainSceneManager.PlayerFollower`, whose Y rotation is the camera's turn; set `PlayerVCamera.PreviousStateIsValid = false` to cut instead of glide.
- Focus menu: free focus reads parallel lists in `GameSettings` (land `_focusTypes`, `_animationStates`, `_propTypes`, camera lists and `_isDesk`; water `..Water` lists plus `_waterDlcPropTypes` and `_isSummerDLC`). Seat spots read their own `FocusAreaSettings` asset. Only `PlayerFocusController` reads these lists. Free focus also needs a ground tag from `ConstData.FocusableTagNames` and distance from the music hill. Within a category every animation is used once (checked in the game data, seat lists included), so category + animation identifies an activity and its prop. 23 of the 49 land activities bring out the personal desk (`_isDesk`); at seat spots the same activities use the furniture instead. The activity row has 11 buttons, each wired in the scene to `UIManager.ButtonSetFocus(n)`.
- Gravity and focus: `SetFocus` turns the `CharacterController` off for every focus, and `PlayerMovementController.Update` skips `Fall()` while focusing, so a player who starts focusing in the air floats there. While the focus menu (or another menu) is open, `Fall()` still runs. FocusAnywhere skips it to hold you in the air. The game's ready checks (`TaskManager.IsReadyForAreaFocus` / `IsReadyForFreeFocus`) need `GroundState.Grounded`. BlueSage QoL's Focus Anywhere forces both to true, so mods copy those checks instead of calling them.
- Personal desk: `PlayerFocusController._desk`, a child of the player, so it sits at a fixed spot in front of them and moving the player moves it for everyone. No code sends it over the network, so other players' games probably show it through the animation (unchecked). DeskSnap tells a friend is at their desk from their synced `AnimState`, and checks whether their desk object is on. Standing up puts you back at `_preFocusPosition` / `_preFocusRotation`, so set those when moving a seated player. While seated the `CharacterController` is off. Who's at a seat spot: `FocusAreaManager.AreaIndexes[NetworkTransforms.IndexOf(net)]` (-1 for none). Desk parts are `MeshRenderer`s under `_desk`, and an empty accessory slot is switched off.
- Instruments: `MusicManager`. Each instrument has 13 keys (0-12). A key's MIDI note is that instrument's octave shift plus the key number. `PlayNoteMaster` / `StopNoteMaster` light the on-screen key and send the note to everyone. The octave arrows (`ShiftOctaveUpMaster` / `DownMaster`) change the octave for the whole lobby. Pressing a key again without stopping it leaves the old note ringing. Music rewards and achievements count time at the instrument, not notes played.
- Saving JSON: don't use Unity's `JsonUtility` for a mod's own classes inside a list or field, because it silently leaves them out (TeleportSpots 0.1.0 saved only `"Version"`). Use Newtonsoft.Json, which the game ships. Store a `Vector3` as plain x, y, z numbers, because Json.NET can't write one directly.
- MIDI files: the game ships Maestro (`MidiPlayer.Run.dll`). `new MidiLoad().MPTK_Load(bytes)` gives `MPTK_MidiEvents`, with note times and lengths in milliseconds.
- Drawing: the easel is automatic. `PlayerFocusController.PaintRoutine` photographs the view and fades it in over 60 seconds. Hand drawing happens on chalkboards and whiteboards (`QuadPainterGPU`): a 320 x 180 grid, 5 chalk colours, and chalk is a shop item that gets used up as you draw. `DrawingManager.ActivateDrawing` moves the `DrawCamera` in front of the board. `QuadPainterGPU.Update` reads the mouse directly, so a mod window over a board needs a patch there or clicks paint the board.
- Setting your name: `PlayerData.Name` (rich text allowed). Closing the ID card (`CustomizationUIController.CloseIDCard`) saves it and, in a world, calls `MainCustomizationController.UpdatePlayerInfo(PlayerData.GetPlayerIdInfo())` (a ServerRpc that relays it to everyone), sets `TextChannelManager.UserName` (chat) and `UIManager.PlayerText`. `GetPlayerIdInfo` sends at most 1000 characters of the name. The ID card copies its name box into `PlayerData.Name` when it closes, so keep `NameInputField.text` in step. Setting a `TMP_InputField`'s `text` from code isn't cut to its `characterLimit` (only typing is).
- Session names: `MultiplayerManager.CreateLobby` creates the lobby with `MainMenuUIController.CreateSessionNameInputField.text`; PurrLobby stores it as Steam lobby data `Name` (case kept), and the lobby list (`LobbyItemController`) and in-game player panel show it with TextMeshPro, so colour tags work. `MainMenuUIController.Update` reads Esc/Enter straight from `Input` (Esc leaves the session list). `MonoSingleton.I` doesn't set itself in `Awake`, so mods catch `MainMenuUIController` / `CustomizationUIController` with a `Start` postfix. The game has its own `FocusType` enum, so write `UnityEngine.FocusType` in IMGUI code.
- Outfits: the game has 3 slots in `PlayerDataZip` (`CustomizationDataIDsDict` / `1Dict` / `2Dict`, item IDs and colour numbers), with `StyleNames` and `SelectedStyleIndex`. Every read and write of the selected slot's outfit goes through the `PlayerDataZip.CurrentCustomizationDataIDs` property, which throws past slot 3 (a save with a bigger `SelectedStyleIndex` won't load without OutfitSlots). `DataManager.LoadPlayerData` (in `Start`, once per game) reads `data.txt` and two backups with `LoadZipDataStatus`, picks one, and sets `DataManager.CustomizationData` (what you wear; spawn sends it) from that property. The save is gzipped Newtonsoft JSON, written by `SavePlayerZipData`, `SaveOldPlayerZipData` and the `SaveBackupZipData` coroutine (also every 180 s in a world). In the outfit screen (`CustomizationUIController`, in both scenes), `ButtonSelectStyle(i)` then `CloseAvatarPanel(true)` is "pick a style and close": it saves, sends the outfit to everyone (`PlayerCustomizationController.ApplyCustomization`) and retakes the ID photo of the screen's preview character. `InitStyleDropdown` runs once, at the end of `Start`. Drinking lemonade (`SetLemonadeHat`) changes and saves the outfit with the screen closed.
- Crash files: on a hard crash Mono writes `mono_crash.<hash>.<n>.json` (same hash = same crash spot; `n` counts up, filling gaps). Its frames are metadata tokens: look them up with Mono.Cecil from `BepInEx\core` (PowerShell 5.1 needs `[Reflection.Assembly]::UnsafeLoadFrom`) and `ModuleDefinition.LookupToken`. The Windows DLLs matched the Mac build's (different MVID, same layout, a call chain that reads through). A frame's `il_offset` is the start of the statement it was running. On the Mac (`"architecture": "amd64"` = Rosetta), the game crashed many times in `BitPacker.ReadBits` while reading a `NetworkTransform` rotation: `ReadBitsWithoutChecks` loads 8 bytes (9 when straddling) through a pointer and masks, read mode keeps no padding, and `PackByteData.Read` makes exact-size arrays, so the last values of every packet are read past the array's end. Hence NetworkCrashFix.
- Flying while seated: while `IsFocus`, `PlayerMovementController.Update` ignores the movement keys and skips `Fall()`, and the game only moves you at stand-up (to `_preFocusPosition`), so a mod can move a sitter every frame. `InputManager.Horizontal` / `Vertical` keep working while seated (eased, rebinding-aware, zeroed while typing). Space stands you up, E is the interact key (chests, instruments, lemonade), F reopens the focus menu. `SetFocus` restarts the camera drift (`StartCameraRotationRoutine`). `FollowScript.Update` (public) moves the camera's follow point to the player; call it after moving them or the camera trails a frame. Area triggers fire on the movement collider, which is off while seated, so flying away leaves the game thinking you're still in the old areas (`PlayerAreaChecker.FocusAreaControllers`, the shop, instruments, chests...) until something sends `OnTriggerExit`. Mod windows with player names: IMGUI always draws on top of uGUI canvases, so a window that mixes TextMeshPro names and IMGUI buttons draws its box on the TMP canvas (DeskSwarm's `WindowCanvas`), not with `GUI.Box`.
- Clickable mod windows (IMGUI) don't block the game. TeleportSpots puts an invisible uGUI `Image` on a top canvas under its window, which stops clicks on game UI and scroll zoom (`PlayerMovementController.Zoom` skips when `InputManager.MouseOverUIObject` is set). It also clears `InputManager.IsMouseButton0Down` so world clicks (`ClickManager`, fishing) don't go through.

## Paths
- Game: `C:\Program Files (x86)\Steam\steamapps\common\On-Together\On-Together`
- Game DLLs: `...\OnTogether_Data\Managed`
- Saves: `%USERPROFILE%\AppData\LocalLow\GigaPuff\On-Together`. `PlayerData.txt` is plain JSON; `data.txt` is compressed.

## Guidelines
- Prefer client-side and cosmetic mods. Avoid anything that changes the economy, tickets, achievements or other players' state in shared lobbies.
- Other installed mod: BlueSage QoL Tweaks (Thunderstore: Blues). It runs compatibility scans of loaded plugins.
